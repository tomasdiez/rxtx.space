On the floor where the router lives there is a small computer that was headed for recycling two years ago. Now it watches what the air, the heat and the sea do around this address, and when something should change what we do, it says so in one plain sentence on Telegram. *Shut the windows, run the purifier.*

![The node's own dashboard: Bayu-Ungasan, Node #01, holding at 6 µg/m³ indoors.]({{ "/assets/img/tx/tx-004-node-dashboard.jpg" | relative_url }})

*The node looking at itself: Bayu-Ungasan, Node #01, holding at 6 in the room, under the line.*

Bali's air is well measured for an island: fifteen public sensors for 4.4 million residents across 5,780 km², and at the one station with a two-year memory, every day since June above the WHO guideline. The visibility problem is being solved, by other people, well. What is not solved is smaller: a family in Kuta Selatan reads that map at 6pm and still doesn't know whether to open the window. The island is measured. The address is not.

The node exists for the address. It reads the house's sensors, the public stations around it, the satellites overhead; the raw readings never leave the building. And it keeps score on itself: its own metric is ρ, the share of alerts that made somebody act. If the messages move no one, the project fails honestly, in its own numbers.

The computer for this already exists. Retired in drawers everywhere, millions of them. An old laptop even brings a battery, so it measures through the storm-season power cuts. The software is free. What costs is keeping the thing alive: every AirGradient deployed in Bali in 2025 went silent within a year, because keeping a sensor truthful is a job and it was nobody's. That is why this lives in Fab City: a workshop within driving distance, enclosures printed in the climate they will live in, Fab Academy to train the installers. The product is not the box.

A month of progress, in versions. The node learned to say *I am here* to the network, and Menorca answered; it is node #2. Updates are signed, and a node that refuses one it cannot verify is doing its job. The local AI model that answers the house on Telegram is a guest: it can read, it cannot change a setting. This week the air purifiers joined as sensors, which is how I know the living room is at twenty-nine degrees as I write this.

Status: two nodes alive, ρ still young. Next, five pilot addresses and ninety days of one weekly question: *did you do anything because of an alert this week?* Code and playbook are open at github.com/fabcity. An old laptop and a window is most of a node.
