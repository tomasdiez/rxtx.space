A book is usually a room you enter after the walls are painted. This one is being built with the door open.

![The Micelio Tropical index: three chapters floating in the map.]({{ "/assets/img/book/micelio-map.png" | relative_url }})

*The book opens as a map, not as a table of contents.*

For months I have been recording memories and turning them into fragments: a street in La Victoria, a factory that closed, a white Robinson BMX in the middle of a national collapse, a mother researching cacao and slavery, a father who crossed the Atlantic with an engineering degree and the suspicion that the future was somewhere else.

It is called **Micelio Tropical**. It is a memoir, but not the kind that arrives finished and polished. It is being written in public, slowly and in sight. *Escrito en público, despacio y a la vista.*

The site is not a table of contents. It is a mycelium map. Each fragment is a node; each node connects by time, by people, by concepts. You can enter through a chapter and come out through a memory you did not know was related. Click a node and the story opens where you are, without changing the address, without ceremony. Underneath, the archive pages still exist, because even an antenna needs a body.

![A fragment from Micelio Tropical: “Una ventana a otro mundo”.]({{ "/assets/img/book/micelio-fragment.png" | relative_url }})

*A fragment with its hifas visible: temporal, personal, conceptual.*

I am not sharing the URL yet. Not because it is secret, but because it needs to breathe before it learns to perform. No countdown, no funnel, no big announcement. When it is ready, some of these fragments will graduate the way transmissions do: rewritten, cited, given a haircut, and filed in the permanent record.

For now: the book exists. It has chapters. It has a map. It is alive.

TX-003, logged from the Bukit in Bali.
